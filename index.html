Two terms used throughout: a lean-back listen is a song that played on by itself (radio, autoplay, or a playlist carrying on) rather than one the listener picked. Lesser-known means in the bottom third of artists in the same genre.

The question changed from "is discovery unfair?" to "why does a fair introduction lead nowhere?" A single global size cut-off would have called 357 of 412 Afrobeats artists lesser-known, so artists are ranked within their own genre.

B1//ProposalNo new screens

Second Listen.

When a liked lesser-known artist hasn't come back after two weeks, bring them back once, with a different song, in the radio, autoplay or mix where the listener first heard them.

B2//MeasurementA/B test

Testing it.

Half of listeners get Second Listen, half don't, chosen at random. Success means more listeners go on to play a different song by the artist 30–90 days later. Today that happens 3.3% of the time.

These are rough units, not a forecast: the 10 listeners here listen far more than most people. Technical detail: from sizing.py, 1.2 triggers per listener a month, baseline 3.26%, 80% power, intra-class correlation planned at 0.02. The 90 days of waiting, not the number of users, set the timeline.

B4//ReflectionLimits

What I learned

  • The most useful moment was the hypothesis failing. Following the data past the first listen gave a sharper, more buildable problem.
  • An all-plays-added-together number nearly misled me. My first draft headlined "skipped less: 7.9% vs 17.8%". A peer review showed that mostly reflects who listens. Now every comparison is within listeners first.
  • Measurement took most of the time. Ranking within genre, checked by hand against Spotify, stopped Burna Boy from counting as "mid".
  • Interviews need neutral questions. Where I raised "popularity bias" myself, the answer isn't used as evidence.

Limits

  • 10 friends, mostly heavy listeners in Nigeria and the UK. One has 27% of the plays.
  • "Lean-back" can't tell Spotify's radio and autoplay apart from the listener's own playlists. "Liked" means not skipped.
  • A "first listen" is the first in the export. Big artists may have been heard before.
  • Size data undercounts African artists. Checked by hand against Spotify for 30 artists, Last.fm undercounts African artists about 3.5× compared with Western ones. It still ranks them in the right order within a genre, so ranking within genre fixes most of it. But artists with no genre tag (71% of lesser-known first listens that played on by themselves) fall back to one fixed Last.fm cut-off, where some African artists may be called lesser-known when they aren't. Deezer, the second check, rates African genres bigger than Last.fm does, which fits that undercount.
  • Observational data can't see recommendations shown but not played.

Spotify already sells artists a way to reach listeners who met them passively. Second Listen does it organically, for every lesser-known artist, in the two weeks that matter.
